Social Security Retirement Benefits: Eligibility and Claiming Age

By MoneyMooring Editorial Team · Updated July 27, 2026 · 9 min readEducational content · Official sources checked July 27, 2026

Editorial disclosure: this guide is educational and is not individual financial, legal, tax or insurance advice. MoneyMooring does not sell or recommend a specific financial product.
Key takeaways
  • Workers can typically qualify for retirement benefits after enough covered work, commonly described as 40 credits or about 10 years.
  • Retirement benefits may start as early as 62, but claiming before full retirement age permanently reduces the monthly amount.
  • Full retirement age depends on birth year and reaches 67 for people born in 1960 or later.
  • Use a personal my Social Security estimate because benefit amounts depend on the worker’s earnings record.

A practical overview of Social Security work credits, early claiming, full retirement age and benefit estimates. The examples below are explanatory, not product quotes or promises of approval, savings, coverage or investment performance.

Eligibility begins with covered work

Social Security retirement benefits are based on work on which Social Security taxes were paid. The SSA states that workers can typically receive benefits after 10 years or more of covered work, but individual eligibility and family benefits can differ.

Claiming age changes the monthly amount

TimingGeneral effect
Before full retirement agePermanently reduced monthly worker benefit
At full retirement ageUnreduced benefit based on the worker’s record
After full retirement age, up to age 70Delayed retirement credits can increase the monthly worker benefit

Full retirement age varies by birth year. The SSA calculator is the authoritative place to confirm it.

Use your own earnings record

Create or sign in to a my Social Security account and review the earnings history. Missing wages can reduce estimates. Compare benefit estimates at several claiming ages alongside other income, health coverage, work plans, survivor needs and longevity—not just the first available payment.

Before filing

  • Confirm the earnings record.
  • Check full retirement age by birth year.
  • Compare estimates at multiple claiming ages.
  • Review spouse, survivor and dependent benefit possibilities.
  • Understand the earnings test if working before full retirement age.
  • Coordinate the first payment with cash reserves and retirement-account withdrawals.

Frequently asked questions

Is full retirement age always 67?

No. It depends on birth year, although it reaches 67 for people born in 1960 or later.

Can I work while receiving retirement benefits?

Yes, but earnings before full retirement age can temporarily reduce current benefits under the earnings test. Use the current SSA rules.

Does waiting always produce more lifetime money?

Waiting can increase the monthly amount, but lifetime results depend on longevity, household benefits, taxes and other circumstances.
