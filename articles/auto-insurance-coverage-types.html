Auto Insurance Coverage Types: Liability, Collision and Comprehensive

By MoneyMooring Editorial Team · Updated July 28, 2026 · 9 min readEducational content · Official sources checked July 28, 2026

Editorial disclosure: this guide is educational and is not individual financial, legal, tax or insurance advice. MoneyMooring does not sell or recommend a specific financial product.
Key takeaways
  • Liability coverage generally addresses injuries or property damage you cause to others, subject to policy limits.
  • Collision generally covers damage to the insured vehicle from a crash; comprehensive generally addresses many non-collision causes.
  • State requirements vary, and a lender or lessor may require physical-damage coverage.
  • Coverage names are not enough—compare limits, deductibles, exclusions and endorsements.

A plain-English guide to major auto insurance coverage categories and the questions to ask under your state’s rules. The examples below are explanatory, not product quotes or promises of approval, savings, coverage or investment performance.

Liability protects against claims by others

Bodily injury liability generally responds to covered injuries you cause, while property damage liability generally addresses covered damage to another person’s vehicle or property. Minimum requirements vary by state and may be far below the potential cost of a serious crash.

CoverageGenerally addressesCommon limitation
CollisionCrash with another vehicle or object, or rolloverDeductible and vehicle value

LiabilityCovered injury or damage caused to othersPolicy limit; does not repair your own car

The policy controls. “Full coverage” is marketing shorthand, not a standardized promise that every loss is covered.

Other coverage to ask about

  • Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage
  • Medical payments or personal injury protection
  • Rental reimbursement
  • Roadside assistance and towing
  • Gap coverage for a financed vehicle

Requirements and availability depend on state law and the insurer.

Build a declarations-page checklist

  • Named drivers and garaging address are accurate.
  • Liability limits are written per person, per accident and for property damage where applicable.
  • Collision and comprehensive deductibles are affordable in cash.
  • Financed or leased vehicle requirements are satisfied.
  • Endorsements and exclusions are read, not assumed.

Frequently asked questions

Is collision coverage required by law?

NAIC materials note that collision is generally optional under state law, but a lender or lessor may require it.

Does comprehensive include every non-crash loss?

It covers many listed non-collision causes, but the contract, exclusions and deductible determine a specific claim.

What does uninsured motorist coverage do?

It can provide protection when a covered loss involves a driver with no insurance or insufficient insurance, subject to state rules and policy terms.
